# Rewriting Patterns

Use these patterns after findings are identified. Do not apply them mechanically; choose the pattern that preserves meaning and reduces unnecessary harm.

## Pattern 1: Replace Label With Person or Specific Role

Use when a word turns a person into a condition, identity, or behavior.

- Before: `酒鬼又爽約了。`
- After: `這位有酒精使用困擾的服務對象未能如期出席。`

Exception: Use identity-first language when a person or community prefers it, such as many Deaf, autistic, blind, or disabled communities in some contexts.

## Pattern 2: Replace Moral Judgment With Observable Behavior

Use when wording blames or shames.

- Before: `他不配合治療。`
- After: `他目前尚未完成治療計畫中的幾個步驟。`

Add context only when supported:
- `可能需要確認交通、費用、語言、照護責任或副作用等障礙。`

## Pattern 3: Replace Stereotype With Role-Neutral or Evidence-Based Language

Use when the text assumes a group role or ability.

- Before: `邀請媽媽們準備點心，爸爸們協助搬重物。`
- After: `邀請照顧者依意願協助準備點心或搬運物資。`

## Pattern 4: Remove Irrelevant Identity

Use when identity is not needed for accuracy.

- Before: `那位女工程師修好了系統。`
- After: `那位工程師修好了系統。`

Exception: Keep identity when it is relevant to representation, discrimination, legal rights, research variables, or a person's chosen self-description.

## Pattern 5: Trauma-Informed Reframe

Use when wording may repeat shame, control, disbelief, or pressure.

- Before: `你必須說清楚當時發生什麼。`
- After: `你可以選擇分享你願意提供的資訊；我們會先說明用途、保密限制與可提供的支持。`

Avoid promising what cannot be guaranteed:
- Before: `你現在完全安全。`
- After: `我們會說明目前可採取的安全措施，也會一起評估你覺得安全的安排。`

## Pattern 6: Historically Loaded Technical Terms

Use when technical language uses avoidable oppressive metaphors.

- `master/slave` -> `primary/replica`, `controller/worker`, `leader/follower`, or another architecture-specific pair.
- `blacklist/whitelist` -> `blocklist/allowlist`, `denylist/allowlist`, `exclude/include list`, or direct verbs like `allow` and `block`.

Compatibility rule:
- If old names exist in code or APIs, preserve them in code font, explain the new term on first use, and propose a deprecation path rather than breaking compatibility.

## Pattern 7: Accessibility and Plain Language

Use when text excludes readers through complexity, jargon, or inaccessible assumptions.

- Prefer short sentences and active voice.
- Explain professional jargon on first use.
- Avoid phrases like `just`, `simply`, or `obviously` when tasks may be difficult for some users.
- Provide alternative channels or formats when instructions depend on sight, hearing, speech, motor control, or high literacy.

## Pattern 8: Source and Preference Caveat

Use when no universal wording exists.

Template:

`我建議先用「X」，但這取決於當事人或社群偏好；若能確認自我稱呼，應以當事人的用語優先。`

Use this for:
- Disability person-first vs identity-first language.
- Indigenous, ethnic, national, migrant, or local community names.
- Survivor/victim terminology.
- Reclaimed slurs or in-group terms.

## Pattern 9: Preserve Harm Without Sensationalizing

Use when the text must discuss violence, suicide, abuse, racism, or oppression.

- Keep enough information for truth, accountability, and support.
- Avoid graphic details unless necessary.
- Avoid a single-cause explanation for complex harm.
- Include support, prevention, rights, resources, or agency when appropriate.
