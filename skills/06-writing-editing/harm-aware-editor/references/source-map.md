# Source Map

This skill can work from user-provided text without browsing. Browse or request current sources when terminology depends on jurisdiction, current official guidance, a specific community's current preference, or a high-stakes professional domain.

## Preferred Source Tiers

1. Official or primary sources:
   - Government public health or civil rights agencies.
   - UN and international organization guidance.
   - Official style guides from relevant organizations.
   - Community-led organizations speaking about their own identities.
2. Professional and specialist sources:
   - Journalism trauma centers.
   - Disability journalism style guides.
   - Medical or behavioral health institutions.
   - Technical documentation style guides.
3. Academic research:
   - Peer-reviewed work on microaggressions, linguistic bias, stigma, trauma, health equity, and stereotype communication.
4. Lived-experience sources:
   - Useful for preference signals and harm descriptions, but do not treat a single post as universal community consensus.

## Known Anchor Sources

- CDC Health Equity Guiding Principles for Inclusive Communication: inclusive, accurate, respectful communication; person-first language; avoid blaming; audience input.
- SAMHSA trauma-informed approach: realize trauma impact, recognize signs, respond through policy/practice, resist retraumatization.
- NCTSN Culture and Trauma: trauma intersects with culture, history, race, gender, location, and language.
- NCDJ Disability Language Style Guide: ask preferred terminology; person-first is not universal; Deaf and autistic communities often prefer identity-first.
- UN gender-inclusive language: avoid discriminatory expressions and stereotypes; use gender-neutral wording when gender is irrelevant.
- UN disability-inclusive language: avoid labels, stereotypes, pity, superhuman framing, and inappropriate disability terms.
- NIAAA / NIDA / SAMHSA substance-use language guidance: avoid stigmatizing terms such as `abuser`, `addict`, and use person-centered, clinically precise language.
- Google developer documentation style guide: replace or write around non-inclusive technical terms; preserve code terms when necessary with clear explanation.
- Microsoft Style Guide: avoid `master/slave`; use architecture-specific alternatives such as `primary/replica`, `primary/secondary`, `controller/worker`.
- GitHub branch renaming guidance: inclusive terminology changes need compatibility handling, redirects, contributor instructions, and migration planning.
- Dart / Global Center for Journalism and Trauma: trauma-informed reporting should minimize further harm and avoid pressuring survivors.
- WHO suicide reporting resource: reporting language can affect prevention; avoid sensationalism and unnecessary method detail.

## Freshness Triggers

Browse before finalizing when:
- The user asks for "latest", "current", "official", "legal", or jurisdiction-specific guidance.
- The topic is a rapidly evolving identity term or reclaimed term.
- The text will be used in healthcare, legal, employment, education, crisis response, or public policy.
- The user requests citations or a formal policy document.

## Citation Rule

When browsing is used, cite sources in the final answer. Distinguish:
- verified source-backed recommendation,
- professional convention,
- community preference that may vary,
- your own contextual inference.
