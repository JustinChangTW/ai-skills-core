# Inclusive and Trauma-Informed Review Rubric

Use this rubric to inspect text before rewriting. A finding must point to a specific phrase, sentence, omission, or framing choice.

## Severity

- `High`: likely to demean, blame, stigmatize, exclude, erase, or retraumatize a person or group in ordinary use.
- `Medium`: may reinforce stereotypes, make unnecessary assumptions, or use avoidable historically loaded terms.
- `Low`: wording is understandable but can be made more precise, respectful, accessible, or context-aware.
- `Confirm`: depends on community, individual preference, jurisdiction, technical compatibility, or exact publication context.

## Risk Categories

### Labeling and Dehumanization

Watch for nouns that reduce people to a condition, behavior, diagnosis, status, nationality, race, disability, or trauma history.

Examples:
- `酒鬼`, `毒蟲`, `精神病`, `殘廢`, `非法的人`
- `the disabled`, `addicts`, `illegals`

Prefer specific and humanizing descriptions:
- `有酒精使用困擾的人`
- `使用輪椅的人` or community-preferred identity-first wording
- `未具特定文件身分的人` only when the legal distinction is relevant

### Blame and Moral Judgment

Watch for language that frames structural barriers, illness, trauma responses, poverty, missed appointments, or limited access as personal failure.

Risk signals:
- `不配合`, `爽約`, `懶`, `不自律`, `自己造成`
- "failed to comply", "refused treatment" without context

Prefer observable descriptions and context:
- `未能完成`, `無法如期出席`, `目前尚未回覆`
- `可能面臨交通、照護、語言或經濟障礙`

### Stereotypes and Essentialism

Watch for statements implying a group naturally shares one trait, job, family role, emotion, ability, morality, or behavior.

Risk signals:
- `女性比較細心`, `男性不會照顧孩子`, `老人都不懂科技`
- Defaulting doctors to men and nurses to women

Prefer specific evidence or role-neutral wording:
- `部分受訪者`, `此研究中的樣本`, `照護者`, `醫療人員`

### Exclusion and Assumptions

Watch for assuming heterosexuality, binary gender, marriage, family structure, native-language ability, citizenship, literacy, disability status, or internet access.

Prefer:
- `伴侶` over `先生/太太` when marital status or gender is irrelevant.
- `家人或支持者` over `父母` when family structure varies.
- Plain language and accessible formats for broad audiences.

### Trauma Retraumatization

Watch for language that pressures disclosure, demands unnecessary details, denies experience, promises impossible safety, or repeats dynamics of shame, control, helplessness, or disbelief.

Risk signals:
- `你怎麼會讓這種事發生`
- `你現在已經安全了` when safety cannot be guaranteed
- Graphic details not needed for the task

Prefer:
- `這不是你的錯`
- `你可以選擇是否回答`
- `我們會說明可提供的支持與限制`

### Historical and Cultural Wounds

Watch for terms tied to slavery, colonization, racial hierarchy, eugenics, institutional violence, forced assimilation, or local histories of oppression.

Examples:
- `master/slave`, `blacklist/whitelist`
- Colonial names or externally imposed labels when a community-preferred name is known

Prefer precise alternatives:
- `primary/replica`, `controller/worker`, `allowlist/blocklist`
- Community-preferred or legally recognized names, with context when necessary.

### Disability and Mental Health

Watch for describing nondisabled people as `normal`, presenting disabled people as inspirational objects, or using disability/mental-health terms as insults.

Risk signals:
- `正常人`, `瘋狂`, `智障`, `受困於輪椅`
- "suffers from" when not chosen by the person

Prefer neutral descriptions:
- `非身心障礙者`, `神經典型者`, `使用輪椅`, `患有/正在經歷/有...` depending on context and preference.

### Precision and Compatibility

Do not rewrite away:
- Direct quotes without noting the edit.
- Legal terms that must remain exact.
- Medical diagnosis terms when clinically required.
- Code identifiers, API fields, command names, or database columns without a migration plan.
- A person's own chosen identity label.

When precision conflicts with inclusivity, preserve precision and add context, explanation, or a forward-compatible naming plan.
